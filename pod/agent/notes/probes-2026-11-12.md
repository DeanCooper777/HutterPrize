up1 showed that with 31 levels and no constraint, QAT spreads the integers (entropy +444 KB per
copy) for −298 KB of archive: a bad trade on average. The open question is the trade at the
margin, when the training itself pays for every weight byte.

- Add a penalty on the coded size of the weights to the training loss: a differentiable estimate
  of each tensor's order-0 integer entropy (e.g. soft assignment of latent/scale to the integer
  bins with a temperature, soft histogram per tensor, entropy in nats, times the tensor's weight
  count), with gradients to the latents and the scales.
- Units, so the penalty is the real trade: archive bytes = loss_nats_per_token × 593.3M / (8 ln 2);
  weights cost in S = 2 × Σ_t N_t·H_t(bits) / 8. In nats/token the penalty is therefore
  λ × 2 × Σ_t N_t·H_t(nats) / 593.3M, with λ = 1 the break-even trade. Because the stand-in
  overstates archive gains about 2×, λ = 2 is the more honest setting. Check my arithmetic.
